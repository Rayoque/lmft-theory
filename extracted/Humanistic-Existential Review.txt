[0000]** Humanistic/Existential Review
[0001]** Client Centered Therapy
[0002]** Views people are always growing and inherently good. Avoids diagnosis, avoid strict adherence to what the ideal person should be. Role of therapist is nondirective, not steering client in any one direction (unlike CBT or systems theory). Driven by what the client wants to talk about. T is a facilitator who is helping the person to grow and change. This occurs by creating unconditional positive regard. Client determines the goals of therapy. Up to the client to decide how many sessions they would like as well, a lot of support for client self-determination.
[0003]** Goals:
[0004]**   self-acceptance and self-actualization (Self-actualization is the process of realizing and expressing your true potential, becoming who you genuinely are, and living in alignment with your values, desires, and inner truth).
[0005]     congruence between idealized self and actual self (living more authentically)
[0006]     increased self-understanding, which can lead to self-acceptance
[0007]     decreased level of defensiveness, insecurity and guilt.
[0008]     more positive relationships and increased comfort with others
[0009]     experience and express feelings of the here and now, improved self-awareness skills.
[0010]** T Role
[0011]     is authentic
[0012]     models authenticity
[0013]     non acceptance
[0014]     nondirective
[0015]     non judgmental approach
[0016]     empathy is essential, through experiencing this empathy, people are able to dive deeper into self understanding and who they are.
[0017]**   Locus of control, client can have more self-awareness about their choices, their experience, having their experience make sense to them. When T is non-judgmental about their experiences, clients can make more sense of their feelings and emotional reactions. More capable of making decisions about how they want to respond.
[0018]**   No clear phases of treatment, T does not really have an agenda, due to it going against the concept of client centered. For the exam, you could have a question that asks you for initial or middle stages with client centered approach. Looking for answers that involved unconditional positive regard, client empathy, T express congruence with their reaction to the client, promotion of self-acceptance (the T can give support for self-acceptance ie when client feels shame), and congruence.
[0019]** Gestalt
[0020]** The idea is that change occurs through increased awareness of here and now experience and dialogic relationship between T and client. Dialogic refers to a therapeutic stance where the therapist and client engage as two authentic human beings — fully present with each other, co-creating the therapeutic process. Heavily influenced by Martin Buber’s “I-Thou” philosophy, where the therapist meets the client as a fellow human, not an object to be treated.
[0021]** Role of Therapist:
[0022]     Authentic present other, showing up as a real person
[0023]     Nondirective
[0024]     Nonjudgemental
[0025]** Present moment awareness.
[0026]** Client will become more aware of what they’re doing, how they are doing it, how they can change themselves, while learning to accept and value themselves.
[0027]** Explaining experience of the client by using description instead of interpretation. Client is crying in session. Interpretation “you seem sad.” For Gestalt phenomenological method: “I see you tearing up, I’m wondering what you’re experiencing right now.” This allows for more expanded of what the client is experiencing. You’re asking them to really describe what they are experiencing in the moment.
[0028]** Therapist presence as a real person allows the other client to become more present – that dialogic relationship. Therapist is open to client experience, back and forth dialogical experience, each person is a real person, genuine curiousity.
[0029]** Experiments in Gestalt: T can make suggestions of client trying something new.
[0030]     Repeating statement: “I’m disappointed in my partner.” T: sit up in your chair, say it a little bit louder. Check in with them about their experience. Client can say no and T can also see how they respond to the suggestion.
[0031]     Paying to client’s present experience as they’re discussing the past: right now, what are experiencing as you think about that past relationship?
[0032]     Empty chair
[0033]     Top dog / underdog dialogue
[0034]     Repeating words of overexaggerating gestures
[0035]     Somebody stands up while saying something
[0036]** Gestalt focuses on body awareness: what are you noticing in your body. Getting in touch with the sensations and associated emotions with the sensations.
[0037]** Focuses on the process: how things are being sad, how the therapy is unfolding, the tons of voice, body language, the energy of the therapy, paying attention to what’s going on in the moment.
[0038]** Existential
[0039]** Change occurs through finding meaning, living authentically and responsibly. The idea that the person can respond, make choices, emphasizes personal choice about how clients want to live their lives. Core question: how do I exist? Inherent meaninglessness to life and existence.
[0040]** Role of Therapist:
[0041]     To be real and authentic other
[0042]** Goals: client discovers their own life meaning, what does life mean to them, confront anxiety inherent in living. Anxiety is a part of the human condition, being able to deal with it effectively instead of getting rid of it. Agency: making their own choices in life, responsibility in the construction of their life. Emphasizes empowerment of the client. Self-awareness. Freedom. Each person has unique identity that can only be known through genuine relationships with others. It’s not until you encounter someone else that you can truly know yourself – this is why T must be a true other. Client experiences their own uniqueness through that. Each person must continually recreate themselves, life is always moving forward, existence is not fixed and we are always changing.
[0043]** Focus on moment to moment during therapy, process not content.
[0044]** Concept of holding: process of looking at the problem, and really holding that, remembering that in the presence of the empathetic other. Our goal is to be with them in their pain, sitting there with them. Naming that emotional pain allows that mutual encounter, the experience is not shared, person is able to now process that situation with increased support.
[0045]** Mastering emotional pain: unique healing activities. Relating to pain as something that you have endured and has shaped who you are, so that the painful experience has meaning and significance. Being able to share your deepest wounds with a present nonjudgemental person – T is there with C in their suffering.
[0046]   Givens in life: death, freedom, meaningless, isolation. Encourages honesty about life’s conditions.
[0047]** “Imaginal death” exercises: Envisioning one's death to reflect on what truly matters. Brings clarity, gratitude, and urgency for authentic living.
[0048]** Experiential / Symbolic Therapy
[0049]   Theory of Change
[0050]** Change happens through the existential encounter – the authentic meeting of the therapist and the client in the present moment and by expanding the client’s range of experience. Very influenced by Gestalt therapy. Generally used with families, started by Carl Whittaker.
[0051]   Role of the Therapist
[0052]     Authentically being with client
[0053]     Playful, creative
[0054]   Treatment Goals
[0055]   The goal is for growth and increased flexibility (people have fixed ways of bring in the world, people can expand their own way of reacting, views of things, interactions).
[0056]   Key Concepts & Interventions
[0057]**   Battle for Structure: Therapist establishes the rules and working atmosphere of
[0058]** treatment. Includes the need for the entire family to be in therapy (a mother and child come in for therapy and there is a question for the initial intervention an experiential therapist would use: the therapist discusses with mom the need to include entire family in therapy would be the correct answer.
[0059]**   Battle for Initiative: Motivation for change must come from family. Includes having a family state the agenda for each session, waiting silently for family to take initiative, allowing family to determine how change is going to happen. The therapist should not be working harder than the family. A family comes in, and nobody is speaking. What would experiential therapist do? The right answer would be the therapist would wait silently for the family to take initiative.
[0060]**   Trial of Labor: Understanding the roles, boundaries, beliefs, history, and levels of conflict within the family. Assessment. Family dynamic.
[0061]**   Activating Constructive Anxiety: Reframing anxiety in the family as efforts toward competence (fear of failing to accomplish what one is capable of). Enhancing motivation, if you identify the positive thing they were afraid wouldn’t happen.
[0062]**   Play, Humor, and “Craziness:” Tapping irrational side. Finding solutions in creative interactions.
[0063]   Phases of Treatment
[0064]   Beginning: Engage family as authentic people. Battle for structure. Encourage all members to attend. Family wins battle of initiative. Gather information about boundaries, coalitions, roles, and level of conflict.
[0065]** Middle: Develop sense of cohesion. Create alternative interactions. Highlight inappropriate boundaries (highlighting when family member pushes their world view on others for example). Role-play situations. Use play and “craziness.”
[0066]   End: Highlight accomplishments and reflect on growth. Identify possible blocks to future
[0067]   growth. Role-play future scenarios. Each member expresses feelings about their experience of therapy.
